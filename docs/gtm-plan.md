# GTM Plan — Gaerkaanooni

Status: draft · Stage: pre-PMF / early · Motion: community-led + founder-led · Budget: bootstrapped (₹0–₹2k/mo)

## 0. Product snapshot

Gaerkaanooni (ग़ैरक़ानूनी) turns the public into the funder of the legal matters people are up
against — whether a matter affects one person or a whole community. Lawyers screen every matter,
citizens fund it, and the platform follows it to judgment — with a registry number and an audited
money trail the whole way. The thesis: *a fair hearing shouldn't depend on what you can afford*.

Two tracks, plus referrals:

- **Campaign** — a legal matter is crowdfunded to a goal. Pledges are only charged if the goal is
  met; if not, every backer is refunded in full.
- **Response fund** — a standing pool for urgent, same-day threats (evictions, demolitions,
  illegal detentions, custodial violence). 25% of any campaign surplus is swept into it.
- **Referrals (/refer)** — an open, account-free intake so a friend, family member, neighbour or
  community group can bring forward a matter for someone who needs a fair hearing but may not have
  asked for help themselves. Dignity-first: never labels a person's circumstances, and contact is
  only stored with consent.

Trust mechanics are the product: lawyer screening, money-back guarantee, 5% platform fee (gateway
carved out of it, never on top), full refunds, integer-paise audited ledger, stage tracking
(`SCREENING → LIVE → FUNDED → ASSIGNED → FILED → IN_PROGRESS → RESOLVED`).

## 0.5 On the name — critical take

The name is the thesis, and it is the strongest asset in this plan — if it is explained.

- **The frame.** *Gaerkaanooni* is what the state keeps doing — evictions without notice,
  unlawful detentions, custodial violence. The platform's answer is not the same lawlessness; it
  is the *law*. Positioning line: **"They act gaerkaanooni. We sue."** The name names the enemy;
  the product is the lawful response. This is reclamation, not lawlessness.
- **The tension.** PIL is a rule-of-law enterprise. A defiant name on a court-respecting product
  can confuse first-time donors ("is this… legal?"). Resolution: **always pair the name with an
  English descriptor** — "Gaerkaanooni — the citizen's court fund" — on the hero, in press
  releases, and in every shared case page. The defiance works only if the lawfulness is
  immediate.
- **Trust becomes load-bearing.** A confrontational name raises suspicion by default. The
  screening / money-back / audited-ledger story is no longer just the product — it is the
  conversion engine. It must lead the hero, the case pages, and the receipts.
- **Design payoff.** The docket/legal-dossier aesthetic plus the name gives a signature move:
  stamp response-track matters **"gaerkaanooni state action — lawful response underway."** The
  stamp does the talking; the registry does the proof.
- **Own the keyword.** "Gaerkaanooni" is a coined term — zero search competition (own it) but
  zero existing search volume. Expect zero organic discovery; brand must be built through press
  and community, not SEO, in year one. Consistent spelling everywhere (see §9) or the keyword
  fragments.
- **The dark matters.** "Custodial violence" and "illegal detentions" are the highest-stakes,
  highest-pushback content the platform will publish. Media framing must be survivor-first, never
  graphic, and ready for establishment backlash — with a standing "we are lawyers operating within
  the law" line (see §10).

## 1. Positioning

**Category.** This is not "crowdfunding for cases" — that's a Ketto clone. It is *citizen-funded
public-interest litigation*: a new category where the public behaves like an organized funder of
constitutional cases, with the rigour of a law firm and the transparency of a public ledger.

**Positioning statement (Moore).**
> For Indian citizens who want their giving to produce real legal outcomes — not just NGO
> fundraising — Gaerkaanooni is a citizen-funded PIL platform that screens every case with
> lawyers, charges you only if the goal is met, and follows the case to judgment. Unlike
> Ketto/Milaap donation pages, we give every matter a registry number, an audited money trail, and
> a public verdict at the end.

**ICP — Donors (demand side).**
- Urban/semi-urban Indians 24–45, salaried or professional, news-literate.
- Already give occasionally (GiveIndia, Ketto, temple/community causes) but are skeptical of
  crowdfunding fraud and NGO opacity.
- Motivated by injustice stories (evictions, pollution, sexual violence, free speech) and by
  seeing a concrete outcome ("your ₹500 helped get the stay order").
- Tilt now skews *activist/anti-establishment-progressive* (the name attracts them). This is good
  for early adoption but narrows the mass market — the English descriptor and trust story are what
  widen it back.

**ICP — Campaigners (supply side).**
- Young lawyers and law-school clinics taking PIL/pro-bono matters but unable to fund court fees,
  drafting, expert reports, or case service.
- Civil-society orgs and community leaders facing eviction/demolition/rights crises.
- Small NGOs that currently beg donors with no case-level accounting.

**Anti-ICP.** Individuals seeking personal/family litigation funding (that's a loan business, not
this). Frivolous or politically-motivated cases. Anyone not willing to publish their matter in a
public registry.

## 2. The hard part: cold-starting a two-sided marketplace

This is a chicken-and-egg business. Donors fund cases; cases attract donors. Sequence matters:

1. **Launch with 2–3 flagship cases chosen for resonance + media hook.** The Noida eviction matter
   (40 families, interim stay) is the ideal launch case: concrete, urgent, emotional, court-visible.
   The Yamuna pollution matter gives the environmental angle. The rape-crisis legal cell gives a
   civil-liberties + gender angle. These three already exist in the seed data.
2. **Use the response fund for early proof.** A dispatched urgent matter produces a same-day
   outcome ("stay granted within 48 hours of the public's money moving") — the single most
   newsworthy thing the platform can do, and it needs no 40-day campaign. Custodial-violence and
   detention matters, however, should *not* be the first public proof points — they carry the most
   risk. Prove with the eviction; build trust with the environment/gender cases; scale into the
   dark matters once the brand has a reputation for due process.
3. **Every backer becomes a distributor.** A donor who funds case № 0002 shares *that case's*
   page, not the platform's homepage. Make case pages aggressively shareable (WhatsApp/OG images, a
   ₹100–₹500 "what this pays for" table).

## 3. Messaging & content

**Message hierarchy.**
- Headline: *They act gaerkaanooni. We sue.* / *You are the funder of the lawful response.*
- Sub: *Screened by lawyers. Charged only if the case reaches its goal. Followed to judgment.*
- Proof points: registry numbers; money-back guarantee; 25% surplus → response fund; public
  ledger; stage updates from the courtroom.
- CTA: *Pledge what you can afford.*
- Always-on line: *"Gaerkaanooni — the citizen's court fund."*

**Content engine** (the brand is already legal-dossier — lean in, now with an edge):
- Case briefs styled as docket entries (matter №, stage stamp, "what your money pays for").
- Follow-the-courtroom updates: hearing dates, orders, next steps — post-launch every case page
  should read like a live case file, not a static donation page.
- Explainer series: "How a PIL actually works", "What a ₹500 pledge buys", "Where does the 5%
  go?" — the last one is itself a trust asset.
- Signature series: **"The unlawful, made lawful"** — one-page "what the state did → what the
  court said → what your money paid for" per resolved matter. This is the name's payoff.
- 2–3 min vertical video per flagship case; reuse stills/typography from the docket aesthetic.
- Hindi + English. The site already types जनहित — double down for WhatsApp-tier reach.

## 4. Channels (ORB)

**Owned (build first):** the site's case pages · a WhatsApp broadcast list for supporters ·
email (announce new matters, hearing outcomes, funding milestones) · referral loop on receipts.

**Rented (drive to owned):**
- Instagram — visual case cards, stage-stamp updates, reel explainers.
- X/Twitter — legal-twitter and legal-aid community (Bar & Bench crowd, law professors, RTI/rights
  activists). Low cost, high relevance for this vertical.
- LinkedIn — lawyers and pro-bono networks (supply side).
- YouTube — long-form case explainers; also feeds SEO.

**Borrowed (shortcut to audiences):**
- Press: Indian Express, The Leaflet, Newslaundry, Bar & Bench, Legally India, and regional press
  for the flagship case's geography (Noida → Delhi/NCR papers). The name itself is a headline —
  pitch it.
- Law-school clinics (NLSIU, NLU Delhi, TISS) — case submissions + pro-bono manpower.
- Civil-society orgs (housing-rights, environmental, women's-rights networks) — co-distribution.
- Legal-activist podcasts and newsletters.

**Sequencing:** pre-PMF → founder-led outreach + press + community (no paid). Once 3+ cases are
live and funded → content/SEO compounding, then a small paid test (₹0–₹2k) on Instagram
retargeting of case-page visitors.

## 5. Launch plan

- **Soft launch (weeks 1–4):** real users only. 3 seed cases live. Instrument everything. Fix
  friction (checkout, login, receipts). Collect every pledge.
- **Beta (weeks 5–8):** open to a waitlist of ~200 early supporters + 10 campaigners. Goal: fund
  the first flagship case end-to-end and publish the first judgment/order as the platform's proof.
- **Public launch (week 8–10):** timed to a court milestone in the flagship case — announce "we
  funded a stay order in 48 hours" as the lead story. Press + WhatsApp + Instagram burst. The
  name is the hook; the money-back guarantee is the reassurance.
- **Post-launch (week 10–14):** share the case outcome, publish the money report (raised → spent →
  order), announce the next two matters. Keep a monthly "docket update" cadence so the platform
  never goes quiet.

## 6. Compliance & trust layer (India-specific, do this before scaling)

- **80G registration (Section 8 company) — the biggest single conversion lever for donors.** Tax
  exemption on donations materially increases pledge size and frequency. Plan the entity +
  registration; treat it as launch-blocking for the full public launch if donors expect receipts.
- Donor receipts (with the itr-india skill for the annual-filing angle).
- Audited money trail is already in the product — publish quarterly summaries.
- Payment: UPI-first via Razorpay (already integrated); card/netbanking as fallback.
- FCRA: only if accepting foreign donors — do not open that door initially.

## 7. Metrics

**North star:** cumulative net funds disbursed to live cases (money that actually reached a
courtroom). Everything else is a means to that.

**Funnel:** visit → sign in → pledge intent → payment captured → case reaches goal → case filed →
judgment.

**Leading indicators (90-day targets):**
- Case supply: 5+ submitted, 3+ live, 1+ fully funded, 1+ reaching an order.
- Demand: 300 engaged supporters (phone-OTP accounts), 100 pledgers, 40% first-pledge recovery
  (pledges that convert because goals are met).
- Trust: 100% refunds honored, 0 fee surprises, 1 published money report.
- Distribution: 3+ press/podcast pickups; case-page share rate (shares per pledge).

## 8. Cadence

- Weekly GTM review (30 min): funnel numbers, experiments, decisions. Keep a written decision log.
- Monthly: money report + docket update published; next two matters announced.

## 9. Open decisions

- **Legal entity + 80G** — who, when, cost. (Highest-leverage open question.)
- **Name mechanics** — lock spelling (recommended: **Gaerkaanooni**, no space, one `ae`; Devanagari
  ग़ैरक़ानूनी); check domain (gaerkaanooni.com/.in), trademark, and any phonetic clash; add the
  English descriptor to every surface.
- **Launch scope** — one flagship case vs. three-in-parallel. (Recommended: three, one per theme.)
- **Budget** — plan assumes ₹0–₹2k/mo; paid retargeting is optional and later.
- **Product rename** — the app's visible copy (hero h1 "PIL Promax", about page, meta, e2e test
  asserting "PIL Promax") still says the old name. Decided but not done: rename user-facing copy
  to Gaerkaanooni (keep internal package names `@pil/*`, DB `pil_promax`).
- **India-only** — INR + Hindi typography already signal it; don't expand geography pre-PMF.

## 10. Risks

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Marketplace cold-start | Seed flagship cases; response fund for same-day proof |
| Name reads as "illegal" → donor distrust | English descriptor + money-back + public ledger on every surface; the name is the thesis, the trust story is the proof |
| Donor distrust / fraud optics | Lawyer screening + money-back + public ledger are the brand |
| Establishment pushback on dark matters | Sequence them late; survivor-first framing; standing "lawyers within the law" line |
| Low pledge recovery (goals unmet) | Refund guarantee is a feature, not a bug — publish refund stats |
| Case supply dries up | Law-school clinics + civil-society partnerships, not paid ads |
| Compliance (80G/entity) stalls | Get the entity decision made in week 1; register before full launch |
